### Elicitation and uncertainty record

The seven clarification areas for a requirements brief are: stakeholders/actors; goals and scope; domain vocabulary/rules; functional workflows; external interfaces/data; quality constraints; acceptance/evidence. Existing specs establish the product owner intent, role families, source fields, six workflows, interfaces and Free boundary. The following remain unresolved for unified integration: final multi-module role matrix and Google identity transition; field ownership/transfer policy after linking; historic record matching/migration authority; production workload and service availability expectations; real provider, hosted host and device acceptance evidence. These are tracked in the unified journey and release plans. Volatility is high for provider quotas, hosted limits, OAuth configuration and user/role mapping; revalidate before release. Comprehension risk is high for cross-module role and shared-field behavior; pin those contracts before implementation.

## NFR priority and trade-offs

| Priority | NFR | Rationale / design trade-off |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Security | Religious/conversion records and contact details require server-enforced least privilege, scoped projections, RLS and explicit grants. This adds SQL policy and validation work; it is a blocking SQA condition. |
| 2 | Integrity | Duplicate people, lost retries, lifecycle and weekly races can corrupt ministry history. Transactional commands and receipts cost implementation complexity but protect canonical records; also blocking SQA. |
| 3 | Availability | Free projects can pause and have no managed PITR. Keep bounded reads and recoverable errors, document manual backups/resume, and accept no service guarantee until a restore rehearsal exists. Redundancy/paid services are outside scope. |
| 4 | Cost | Capacity is quota-bound and workload is not yet measured. Keep reads and media bounded and monitor usage; preserve the Free-tier boundary even if that requires reducing workload. |
| 5 | Maintainability | Small feature/domain/adapter boundaries support the next integration and review. Favor composition and typed ports; do not trade security for a broad shortcut or refactor the completed modules without a demonstrated need. |

## Blocking SQA and tracked SQC

**SQA blockers (stop a release):** any cross-scope read/write, direct client business mutation, PII leak in route/log/error, failed receipt replay/idempotency or race, broken lifecycle/weekly invariant, unauthorized Storage access, missing source-hash parity, or unreviewed migration/restore/cutover. Prevention: schema constraints, grants/RLS, narrow RPCs, pure validation, scoped projections and owned migrations. Appraisal: local native-PG tests, malicious/invalid partitions, analyzer/review, live Auth/Storage and hosted/device release evidence. Failure cost: private religious data exposure, duplicate/missing identities, corrupt attendance/history or an unrecoverable cutover.

**SQC (track and prioritize):** responsive layout, text scaling, keyboard order/focus, accessible names/outcomes, reduced-motion response, contrast and heuristic issues. Prevention: shared components, semantics and tested responsive patterns. Appraisal: automated widget checks plus manual keyboard/screen-reader/contrast review at target widths. Failure cost: staff exclusion, slower workflows and missed errors. SQC findings do not waive an SQA blocker.
